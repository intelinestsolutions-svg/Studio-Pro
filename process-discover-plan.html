Why this stage exists

Planning is where the money is saved.

Most website projects go wrong before design starts — with a vague idea, a hidden scope and a price that grows. This stage removes all three.

01

Clarity before cost

You approve exactly what is being built and what it costs before any build work begins. No open-ended hourly billing, no surprise invoices at the end.

02

Built for your customer

We map who you are selling to, what they search for, and the shortest path from landing on your site to sending you an enquiry.

03

SEO decided before the sitemap

Keyword research happens before we agree the pages — so every page has a job in Google, and you are not paying later to restructure the site.

04

One document, one truth

The blueprint becomes the single source of truth for design, build, copy and testing — so nothing gets lost between a chat message and the final site.

Rule 01One decision-maker

Feedback comes from one person with authority, so the project never stalls in committee.

Rule 03Price locked at sign-off

Rule 04You own everything

Step by step

Six steps, in plain order.

Each step has a job, a deliverable and a moment where you approve it before we move on.

01

Kickoff & discovery workshop

Day 1 · 60 min

We start with a conversation, not a form. In one focused session we pin down what the website must actually do for your business — and how we will know it worked.

What we do

  • Run a 60-minute workshop (video or in person in Kota Kinabalu)
  • Define goals and success measures — enquiries, calls, bookings, sales
  • Sketch your audience: who they are, what they search, what stops them
  • Check your current site, domain, hosting and analytics

What you receive

  • Written project brief (1–2 pages) in your language
  • Agreed success measures you can measure later
  • List of risks and assumptions, stated openly
  • Confirmed contacts, roles and decision-maker

What we need from you

  • Your goals in plain words — what a “good month” looks like
  • Logins for your current site, domain or analytics (if you have them)
  • 3 websites you like, and one thing you dislike about each
02

Research & benchmarking

Day 1–3

Before we design anything, we look at what your customers already see — your competitors, your market, and the searches happening in your area.

What we do

  • Review 3–5 competitors: structure, offers, weaknesses
  • Study your existing content and analytics (if available)
  • Collect a moodboard of styles that fit your brand
  • Pinpoint your positioning — why someone should choose you

What you receive

  • One-page competitor & opportunity summary
  • Positioning notes you can reuse in ads and social
  • Two visual directions to choose between

What we need from you

  • Your main competitors (or let us pick them)
  • What genuinely makes you different
  • Any current offers, packages or promotions
03

Sitemap & content architecture

Day 2–4 Cheapest stage to change your mind

We decide every page, its purpose, and the shortest path from a visitor landing to you getting the enquiry — before any layout is designed.

What we do

  • Build the full page list — and agree what to cut
  • Design the main menu, footer and page hierarchy
  • Map the visitor journey and where each call-to-action sits
  • Set URL structure (clean, SEO-friendly, redirect-ready)

What you receive

  • Sitemap diagram you can edit with us
  • Purpose statement for every page
  • Navigation structure and URL list
  • Enquiry path: form, WhatsApp, call — decided here

What we need from you

  • Approval of the sitemap before wireframes start
  • Anything you definitely want included (or excluded)
04

UX wireframes

Day 3–7 The core of this stage

Grey boxes and lines first. Deciding the layout here costs nothing — moving things around after visual design and code costs days. You get a prototype you can click through on your own phone.

What we do

  • Low-fidelity wireframes for every key template
  • Desktop and mobile versions of each screen
  • Place headlines, CTAs, forms, testimonials and trust signals
  • Build a clickable prototype and check accessibility basics

What you receive

  • Clickable prototype link (open it anywhere)
  • Annotated PDF of each screen, desktop + mobile
  • Notes explaining every layout decision

What we need from you

  • One consolidated feedback list within 48 hours
  • Feedback from one decision-maker only
05

SEO & content mapping

Day 5–8

We agree what each page should rank for and what it should say — so writing and building never has to be redone later.

What we do

  • Keyword research for your services, city and area
  • Assign one primary keyword to every page
  • Outline headlines and sections per page (copy skeleton)
  • Plan titles, meta descriptions and structured data

What you receive

  • Keyword map — page by page
  • Content outline you (or we) can write from
  • Meta title & description drafts
  • Option to add copywriting to your project

What we need from you

  • Final service names and service areas
  • Prices, FAQs, real photos and case examples
06

Scope, timeline & sign-off

Day 8–10 Approval gate

Everything above becomes a binding plan: what is included, what it costs, who does what, and the date it ships. You sign once, then we build.

What we do

  • Final scope document — an in / out list, clearly written
  • Fixed quote: one price for the agreed scope
  • Milestone schedule with real dates
  • Risk list and the written change-request process

What you receive

  • Blueprint pack: brief, sitemap, wireframes, keyword map, scope, quote, schedule
  • Your named point of contact
  • Confirmed kick-off date for Stage 02 — Design & Build

What we need from you

  • Written approval of the blueprint
  • Deposit to lock your place in the schedule

Working together

What we need from you.

This stage is fast because it is predictable. Six things from your side keep the 10 days on track.

Your side of the desk

Everything below — nothing more.

  • One decision-maker who can approve without a committee
  • Your brand assets — logo and colours, or a brief for us to create them
  • Existing material — content, photos, price lists, brochures
  • Access to domain, hosting and analytics (we guide you through it)
  • One feedback list per review — consolidated, within 48 hours
  • Answers within 1 business day while the stage is running

The 48-hour rule

Why we ask, and what happens if it slips.

Design and planning are decision work. Every day a review waits, the whole schedule waits with it — which is why we keep reviews short, focused and time-boxed.

  • You get 48 hours per review — usually plenty, because we send a prototype, not a 40-page PDF.
  • We reply within 48 hours too — questions, clarifications, quick calls.
  • If feedback is late, the schedule moves day-for-day and we confirm the new dates in writing.
  • New ideas are welcome — anything outside the agreed scope gets a written note on price and timing before we start it.
Questions

Before you begin.

The things clients ask us most about the planning stage.

How long does Discover & Plan actually take?
Most projects take 7–10 working days from kickoff to sign-off. Larger sites, e-commerce or multi-location businesses can take up to two weeks. The clock starts when we have your answers from the kickoff workshop — the schedule moves day-for-day if content or approvals arrive late, and we confirm new dates before that happens.
Do I need to have my content ready first?
No. You only need enough to describe what you offer. During Step 5 we produce a content outline for every page — the headlines, sections and points each page must make. If you would rather not write it, ask us to add copywriting to your project and we will draft it for you.
I'm not sure what I want. Is that a problem?
That is exactly what this stage is for. You bring the business goal (“more bookings”, “fewer phone calls asking the same questions”, “look as established as our competitors”) and we turn it into a structure. You never have to arrive with a sitemap — you approve the one we propose.
Is this stage free?
The first discovery call is free and carries no obligation. The full blueprint is part of your project quote when you continue with us — you are not billed twice. If you only want the planning work and no build, we can quote Stage 01 on its own as a fixed fee.
Can I change the sitemap after I approve it?
Yes — and the earlier the better. During Stage 01 changes are cheap because nothing has been designed or coded yet. Once you sign off, changes still happen, but they go through the written change-request process so you see the effect on price and timing first. Nothing is ever added silently.
I already have a website. What happens to it?
We audit it during Step 2, keep whatever still works, and build a URL map with 301 redirects in Step 3 so you do not lose existing rankings or bookmarks when the new site goes live. Your old site stays online until the new one is approved and tested.
How many revisions are included?
Sitemap and wireframes are iterated until you are happy — that is the point of doing them before design, and it costs us very little. Visual design (Stage 02) comes with a defined number of revision rounds, written into your quote so you know exactly what is covered.
What happens the moment I sign off?
We confirm your slot in the schedule, issue the invoice for the agreed deposit, and start Stage 02 — Design & Build: visual direction from the moodboards you picked, then full page designs, then development against the approved wireframes. Your point of contact and weekly update day are already set.
Do I own the files and the plan?
Completely. The brief, sitemap, wireframes, keyword map and every design file are yours, handed over at the end of the stage — and you keep them even if you decide not to build the site with us.
Can you do this stage remotely?
Yes. We work with clients across Sabah, Malaysia and overseas. The workshop runs on video, wireframes are shared as clickable links, and reviews happen over email or WhatsApp. If you are in Kota Kinabalu, we are happy to meet in person instead.
