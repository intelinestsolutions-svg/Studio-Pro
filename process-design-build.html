Why this stage exists

This is where the plan becomes something you can click.

Stage 01 removed the guesswork. Stage 02 removes the risk — nothing is built until you have seen and approved it.

01

Approved before it is built

You sign off the design in writing before a line of code is written. If something is wrong, it costs an edit — not a rebuild.

02

A system, not a one-off

Buttons, cards, forms and menus are designed once and reused everywhere — so page 12 looks as considered as your homepage.

03

Speed designed in, not patched on

Image budgets, lazy loading, clean code and caching are decisions we make while designing — not a repair job after launch.

04

Built so you can run it

A proper CMS, a training session and a written guide — daily updates never need a developer or a support ticket.

Rule 01Two revision rounds

Round one fixes structure, round two fixes polish. Anything beyond that becomes a written change note.

Rule 02A preview link every week

You never wonder what is happening — each week you get a link you can click through.

Rule 03Staging first, always

Your live site is never used as a test area. Everything is rehearsed on a private staging address.

Rule 04Approved design = build to spec

Development follows the signed-off designs exactly, so the finished site matches what you approved.

Step by step

Six steps, in plain order.

Design comes first and is approved, then build. Each step ends with something you can look at.

01

Visual direction & moodboard

Day 1–3

Your blueprint told us what to build. Now we decide how it should feel — two clearly different directions, you choose one.

What we do

  • Turn the Stage 01 directions into two full moodboards
  • Define palette, type scale, imagery and icon style
  • Check contrast and accessibility from the very first pixel
  • Align with your existing brand — or establish one

What you receive

  • Two visual directions, each with a short rationale
  • Your chosen palette and typography scale
  • Reference board you can reuse in print and social

What we need from you

  • Pick one direction within 48 hours
  • Your logo and brand colours (or approval to create them)
  • Anything you never want the site to look like
02

Homepage design

Day 3–7

The homepage sets the tone for everything else. We design it fully — desktop and mobile — and get your approval before the rest of the site follows.

What we do

  • Design the complete homepage, desktop and mobile
  • Apply headline and call-to-action hierarchy from the wireframes
  • Add trust elements: testimonials, stats, badges, guarantees
  • Plan the motion — what moves, when, and why

What you receive

  • Homepage designs you can open and comment on
  • The matching mobile design
  • Notes on animation and interaction behaviour

What we need from you

  • One consolidated feedback list within 48 hours
  • Final headline wording, or approval of ours
03

Every page template & the UI system

Day 6–11

Every remaining template is designed as part of a reusable system, so the site looks intentional twenty pages in — not improvised.

What we do

  • Design each remaining template: service, about, contact, gallery, blog
  • Build the UI kit — buttons, cards, forms, menus, footer, icons
  • Apply the system across all breakpoints
  • Accessibility pass: contrast, focus states, tap-target sizes

What you receive

  • Designs for every template, desktop + mobile
  • The component library (UI kit) the site is built from
  • Consistency check report across pages

What we need from you

  • Photos and content for the key pages (we can source stock)
  • Final service names, packages and offers
04

Revisions & design sign-off

Day 11–13 Approval gate

Two structured revision rounds, then the design is locked — so development starts with zero guesswork and zero rework.

What we do

  • Round 1: structure, wording and section changes
  • Round 2: polish, spacing, colour and detail
  • Final consistency and accessibility check
  • Document any difference from the Stage 01 blueprint

What you receive

  • The final approved designs
  • A revision log showing every change you asked for
  • Written confirmation that the build starts

What we need from you

  • Consolidated feedback per round, within 48 hours
  • Approval from one decision-maker
  • Written sign-off to lock the design
05

Development & performance build

Day 13–19

Now we code it: semantic, responsive, quick to load and easy for Google to read — a hand-built site, not a stack of plugins.

What we do

  • Build every template to the approved designs
  • Responsive at each breakpoint, with your animations
  • Performance work: compressed images, lazy loading, caching
  • SEO-ready markup, forms, SSL and spam protection

What you receive

  • A private staging link to click through
  • Your speed test results
  • Short technical notes on what was built

What we need from you

  • Answers to technical questions within one business day
  • Patience for build week — progress arrives as one preview
06

CMS, staging rehearsal & training

Day 17–20

You get a site you can actually run — with a full rehearsal, and a training session, before the public ever sees it.

What we do

  • Set up the CMS: pages, posts, menus, forms, settings
  • Load your content and run a full staging rehearsal
  • Click through every page, form, link and menu — fix the list
  • Run a 45-minute training session and record it

What you receive

  • Staging site for your own final check
  • Your CMS login and a short editing guide
  • Recorded training you can rewatch any time

What we need from you

  • Test it on your own phone and laptop
  • Report anything odd within 48 hours
  • Nominate the person who will edit the site

How changes are handled

New ideas are welcome — surprises are not.

Everything inside the signed-off blueprint is simply done. Anything outside it gets a short written note first, showing the effect on price and timing, so you decide with full information.

  • In scope — done immediately, no discussion needed.
  • Small tweak — absorbed into the round you are already in.
  • New idea — written impact note on cost and days, your call before we start.
  • Too big for now — parked on a wish list and quoted as Stage 03 work.
Questions

Before the build begins.

What clients ask us most about design and development.

How long does Design & Build take?
A typical business site takes 15–20 working days (about 3–4 weeks) from first moodboard to a finished site on staging. E-commerce, bookings or custom features take longer — your exact number is written into the Stage 01 scope, so you know before you commit.
Do you design every page, or use a template?
Every template is designed for you — homepage, service pages, about, contact, and whatever else your sitemap needs. We do not drop your content into a bought theme. Reused components (buttons, cards, forms) are deliberate: they keep the site consistent and fast.
How many rounds of changes are included?
Two structured rounds — one for structure and wording, one for polish — plus unlimited small fixes for anything we got wrong. Requests beyond the agreed scope are always quoted in writing first, so there is never a surprise line on the invoice.
Can I watch it being built?
Yes. You get a preview link every week, and once development starts you receive a private staging address you can open any time. Nothing is hidden behind a “wait for the reveal” — you see progress as it happens.
Will I be able to edit the site myself?
Yes. The site runs on a CMS with clearly labelled fields for text, images, pages and posts. We set it up, load your content, run a 45-minute training session (recorded) and give you a short written guide. Most clients publish their first update the same day.
Will it work on phones?
Every design is created desktop and mobile side by side, then tested on real devices — iOS and Android — and in Chrome, Safari, Firefox and Edge. Your customers are mostly on phones; so are our priorities.
How fast will it be?
Speed is built in: compressed and properly sized images, lazy loading, caching, minimal scripts and clean hand-written code. We show you the measured results before launch, and we aim for “opens instantly on 4G”, not just a green tick.
What if I want something that is not in the blueprint?
Ask — usually we can. If it is inside the signed-off scope it simply happens. If it is new, you get a one-paragraph note showing the cost and the effect on the launch date before any work starts, and you decide.
Who owns the design files and the code?
You do. Design files, the component library and the site's source code are handed over at the end of the stage. If you ever want to work with another developer, nothing is locked or licensed against you.
What happens if I skip Stage 01?
You can, but design becomes slower and more expensive: every decision that should have taken one conversation gets made during the build, at designer and developer rates. The blueprint is what lets us hold a fixed price and a fixed date in this stage.
